# Scoring, synthesis and decisions

## Score every interview (1–5 each)

| Score | Pain | Urgency | Budget | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Doesn’t see a problem | “Someday” | No budget, no path to one | No influence on purchases |
| 3 | Sees the problem, no workaround yet | Within 1–2 years | Could find budget if pushed | Influences the decision |
| 5 | Actively hurting, already spending time or money on a workaround | This year: audit finding, board ask or incident | Named budget line or recent comparable purchase | Signs or directly recommends |

**16–20:** strong pilot candidate. Follow up within a week about being a design partner.
**11–15:** interested. Keep warm, send the summary, recheck in 2–3 months.
**10 or less:** not a buyer now, but often a good source of referrals.

## Decision rules after 30 interviews

Agree on these **before** starting, so the results can’t be rationalized after the fact.

| Result | What it means | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| 3+ pilot letters of intent, and H1–H3 supported | Real, urgent pain with a buyer | Build the prototype; start the pre-seed raise |
| Strong pain, but “not for 2+ years” | Right problem, early timing | Narrow to whatever is urgent now (e.g. AI-driven attack detection or CIP-015 support); apply for SBIR/DOE grants to fund the wait |
| Pain is about AI-driven **attacks**, not AI **inside** OT | Different wedge | Re-center the product on detecting machine-speed attacks; update the deck |
| Pain exists but only at large investor-owned utilities | Beachhead is wrong | Rework go-to-market for longer cycles and partner-led sales |
| Most Dragos/Claroty/Nozomi users say their tool already covers AI | Incumbents own it | Narrow to the approval/hold-point workflow and vendor-AI certification, or pursue partnership/acquisition early |
| Little pain anywhere | The market isn’t here yet | Stop, or pivot to an adjacent problem that came up repeatedly in interviews |

## What a pilot letter of intent looks like

A short, **non-binding** letter that says the utility intends to run a pilot. It isn’t a contract, but it shows investors real demand. Have a lawyer review the template before using it.

> **[Utility letterhead]**
>
> [Date]
>
> [Utility] intends to evaluate [Company]’s AI security platform in a pilot at [site or environment], beginning approximately [quarter, year], for approximately [duration].
>
> The pilot is expected to run in observe-only mode and to focus on [discovering AI tools that reach operational systems / producing audit evidence / other]. [Utility] expects to provide [access, staff time, feedback sessions] and anticipates a pilot fee of approximately $[amount], subject to final agreement.
>
> This letter expresses intent only and does not create a binding obligation on either party.
>
> [Name, title, signature]

## Share what you learn

After 30 interviews, write a 2-page anonymized summary (“What 30 utility security leaders told us about AI in OT”) and send it to everyone who talked to you. It keeps them warm, earns referrals, and becomes marketing content later.
