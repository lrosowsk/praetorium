# Who to talk to, and where to find them

**Target:** 30 conversations in 6 weeks. Aim for about 20 with buyers and 10 with influencers and experts.

## Mix

| Segment | Interviews | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Electric co-ops (distribution and G&T) | 8 | Beachhead. Faster decisions, under-served by enterprise vendors |
| Municipal / public power utilities and joint action agencies | 8 | Beachhead. Same reasons; joint action agencies serve many members at once |
| Investor-owned utilities | 4 | Bigger budgets; shows whether the pain is the same at scale |
| OT security vendors, integrators, consultants | 4 | See many utilities; future partners or channels |
| NERC CIP auditors / compliance consultants | 3 | Know what evidence regulators will ask for |
| National labs and researchers (INL, PNNL, others) | 3 | Credibility, grant partners, technical reality check |

## Roles to target

- **Primary buyers:** CISO, OT security manager, director of cybersecurity, IT/OT director.
- **Influencers:** NERC CIP compliance manager, SCADA/EMS manager, control center manager, substation automation engineer.
- **At small co-ops and municipal utilities**, the IT manager or general manager often owns security. Ask for whoever handles cybersecurity.

## Where to find them

**Industry associations**
- **APPA** (American Public Power Association): public power utilities. Cybersecurity programs and events.
- **NRECA** (National Rural Electric Cooperative Association): electric co-ops. Runs cybersecurity programs for members.
- **State and regional associations** of co-ops and municipal utilities: smaller, friendlier events and newsletters.
- **Joint action agencies** (public power) and **G&T cooperatives**: one relationship reaches many member utilities.

**Information sharing and government**
- **E-ISAC** (Electricity Information Sharing and Analysis Center): the grid security community. Its events draw exactly the right people.
- **DOE CESER** (Office of Cybersecurity, Energy Security, and Emergency Response): programs, funding calls and events.
- **National labs:** Idaho National Laboratory and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory run grid and ICS security research and training.

**Conferences**
- **S4** (ICS security), **DistribuTECH / DTECH** (utility technology), **SANS ICS Summit**, **ICS Village** at security conferences, regional co-op and public power conferences.
- Use agendas and speaker lists as target lists: speakers on OT security are often happy to talk.

**Online**
- LinkedIn searches: “OT security” + “electric cooperative”, “NERC CIP” + “compliance manager”, “SCADA” + “municipal utility”.
- Utility websites often list leadership, and co-ops publish board and staff directories.

## Warm intros beat cold email

Cold outreach to utilities gets low response. Before cold-emailing, list everyone you know who might know a utility person: former colleagues, alumni networks, investors, local co-op board members, security community contacts. Ask each interviewee for two more names. Referral chains do most of the work after the first five calls.
