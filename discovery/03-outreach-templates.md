# Outreach templates

Keep every message short, specific and clearly **not a sales pitch**. Utility security people get vendor spam constantly; being a researcher asking for advice is what gets replies. Replace everything in [brackets].

## 1. Cold email

**Subject:** Question on AI and OT security at [Utility]

> Hi [First name],
>
> I’m [your name], researching how utilities are handling AI tools showing up in and around operational systems: vendor copilots in SCADA and EMS software, engineers using AI agents, and attackers using AI too.
>
> I’m not selling anything. I’m talking to 30 utility security and compliance leads to understand what’s real and what’s hype, and your perspective from [a co-op / public power / their specific context] would be really valuable.
>
> Would you be open to a 25-minute call in the next couple of weeks? Happy to share what I’m hearing from peers in return.
>
> Thanks,
> [Name]
> [One line of credibility: background, or who referred you]

**Variant if you saw them speak or post:**
> I caught your talk on [topic] at [event] / your post about [topic], especially the point about [specific thing]. …

## 2. LinkedIn connection note (under 300 characters)

> Hi [First name], I’m researching how utilities handle AI tools reaching OT systems. Not selling anything; I’d value 25 minutes of your perspective as [title] at [Utility]. Open to a quick call?

## 3. Follow-ups

**Follow-up 1 (4–5 business days later, reply in the same thread):**
> Hi [First name], bumping this in case it got buried. Even 15 minutes would help. If you’re not the right person, could you point me to whoever handles OT security at [Utility]?

**Follow-up 2 (one week later, last one):**
> Hi [First name], last note from me. I’ll be sharing a short summary of what I learn from about 30 utility security leads. Happy to send it to you either way; just reply “send it.”

The “send it” offer often gets replies even from people who won’t take a call. Then follow through.

## 4. Asking a contact for a warm intro

Make it easy: send a forwardable blurb.

> Hi [Contact],
>
> Do you know anyone in security or compliance at an electric utility, especially a co-op or municipal utility? I’m researching how utilities handle AI tools reaching their control systems and am looking for 25-minute advice calls. Not selling anything.
>
> If someone comes to mind, here’s a blurb you can forward:
>
> > *[Your name] is researching how utilities are handling AI in and around operational systems: vendor AI features, engineers’ AI tools and AI-driven attacks. They’re talking to utility security leads to learn what’s real. 25 minutes, no sales pitch. Interested?*

## 5. After the call: thank you and referral ask (same day)

> Hi [First name],
>
> Thanks again for your time today. The point about [specific thing they said] was really useful. It’s changed how I think about [X].
>
> You mentioned [name/role] might be worth talking to. Would you be comfortable making an intro? I can send a short blurb if that helps.
>
> I’ll send you the summary once I’ve finished these conversations.
>
> [Name]

## 6. At a conference

Opener for hallways and receptions:
> “I’m researching how utilities are dealing with AI showing up near their control systems. Is that on your radar at all, or is it still hype?”

Then listen. Get their card or LinkedIn and ask for a 25-minute follow-up call. Send the follow-up within 24 hours, referencing where you met.

## Cadence

- Send 10–15 new outreach messages a week. Expect a low reply rate on cold messages and much higher on warm intros.
- Block interview slots in advance (e.g. Tuesday to Thursday, 10am–3pm in the interviewee’s time zone).
- Log everything in `target-list.csv` the same day.
